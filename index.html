Hi, I'm Junsung. I study how vision-language and language models use what they see and read, and how to make them more reliable.

I'm a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at Seoul National University, advised by Prof. Sungroh Yoon, and I expect to graduate in August 2028. I did my undergraduate here too.

I work on vision-language models and large language models. I use interpretability to find what a model actually relies on, and post-training and synthetic data to make it more reliable.

Next, I want to dig deeper into post-training for vision-language models. If you'd like to talk about research or a summer 2027 internship, I'd be glad to hear from you.

Heads That Write, Not Just Point: Image Retrieval Heads in Vision-Language Models

Junsung Park, Sangwon Yu, Donghun Kang, Yeongtak Oh, Jaewon Jeong, Gyeongtae Yoo, Han Cheol Moon, Jungbeom Lee, Sungroh Yoon

When a vision-language model answers a question about one image among many, which attention heads find that image? We separate pointer heads, which place attention on the target image, from writer heads, whose outputs carry enough information to identify it. Across four open-weight models, only 35 to 47 of the top 100 heads are selected by both criteria, and ablations with random-head controls show that standard inference is most sensitive to those heads.

Know “No” Better: A Data-Driven Approach for Enhancing Negation Awareness in CLIP

Junsung Park, Jungbeom Lee, Jongyoon Song, Sangwon Yu, Dahuin Jung, Sungroh Yoon

CLIP often fails to tell a concept from its negation, such as “parking” from “no parking.” An analysis of public pre-training data points to a shortage of captions with negation, so we generate such captions with an LLM and a multimodal LLM and fine-tune CLIP's text encoder on them. NegationCLIP improves negation understanding while preserving zero-shot classification and retrieval performance, and its text encoder also helps text-to-image generation and referring image segmentation without further training.
